Coordinate frames and pose conventions

Follow a point from the camera to the IMU and world, with explicit transform directions and units.

Prerequisites: Camera and IMU measurement models

A vector needs a frame

The numbers [1, 0, 0] describe a direction only after its coordinate frame is known. A point one meter in front of a camera is different from a point one meter along an IMU's x-axis. In these lessons, W is a local world frame, I an IMU frame, and C a camera frame. These letters name coordinate systems; they do not imply an axis convention.

ROS commonly uses x forward, y left, and z up for a body frame, while a camera optical frame uses x right, y down, and z forward. A dataset or estimator can use different conventions. Read its definitions before copying a transformation.

Define the direction once

We write T_AB for the rigid transform taking coordinates from frame B into frame A. R_AB is its rotation; t_AB is the location of B's origin expressed in A. All point coordinates and translations below are in meters. A transform stored as T_cam_imu in Kalibr maps IMU coordinates into camera coordinates.

Composition follows the intermediate frame: T_WC = T_WI T_IC. The rightmost operation acts first. To use Kalibr's T_CI in this expression, first invert it to obtain T_IC.

p_A = R_AB p_B + t_AB; T_BA = [R_ABᵀ, −R_ABᵀ t_AB; 0, 1]

Check rotation and position separately

An inverse transform requires both transposing the rotation and rotating the negative translation. Negating translation alone is generally wrong. Verify a conversion with a simple point: map the source origin into the destination frame, then map it back. The round trip should recover the original point within numerical precision.

Quaternions also require a convention. Record component order, the direction of the represented rotation, and the multiplication convention. A four-number array is not a complete orientation specification.

Know what a trajectory tracks

A trajectory may describe the IMU, a camera, or a ground-truth marker. Rigidly attached sensors still follow different position paths while rotating because their origins are separated. Convert estimates and references to the same physical frame before evaluation. Also distinguish a locally continuous odometry frame from a globally corrected map frame.

WORKED EXAMPLE

If T_WI maps IMU coordinates into world coordinates and T_IC maps camera coordinates into IMU coordinates, how do you map a camera point into the world?

Explanation

Apply T_IC first, then T_WI: T_WC = T_WI T_IC. The intermediate IMU frame matches, so the composition is valid.
