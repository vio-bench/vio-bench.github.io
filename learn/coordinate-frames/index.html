Coordinate frames and pose conventions

Global, IMU, and camera frames; rigid-transform direction; and the OpenVINS JPL quaternion convention.

Prerequisites: Camera and IMU measurement models

Frame labels and axes

G denotes a local global frame, I an IMU frame, and C a camera frame. R_AB maps vector coordinates from B to A; this is the destination–source convention used throughout these lessons. OpenVINS writes the global-to-IMU rotation as ᴵ_G R and stores the corresponding quaternion together with the IMU position expressed in G.

A frame label does not specify its axes. ROS body frames commonly use x forward, y left, z up, while optical frames use x right, y down, z forward. Check the actual dataset and sensor definitions. Positions and translations here use meters, and angles use radians unless stated otherwise.

Rigid transforms and composition

T_AB maps a point from B to A. Its translation t_AB is the position of B’s origin expressed in A. Kalibr’s T_cam_imu is T_CI: it maps IMU coordinates into camera coordinates. This matches OpenVINS camera extrinsics R_ItoC and p_IinC.

To map a camera point into G, use T_GC = T_GI T_IC, with the rightmost transform acting first. Invert T_CI to obtain T_IC. An inverse requires a transposed rotation and a rotated negative translation; negating translation alone is generally incorrect.

JPL quaternion convention

OpenVINS uses a unit JPL quaternion q̄ = [q_vᵀ, q₄]ᵀ, with three vector components followed by the scalar. Component order alone is insufficient: its algebra has ij = −k, whereas Hamilton algebra has ij = k. This algebraic convention does not change the handedness of the coordinate frames.

The equation gives the OpenVINS rotation matrix, with [q_v]× v = q_v × v. For the IMU state it represents R_IG, from G to I. OpenVINS uses a left-multiplicative orientation error: q̄ = δq̄ ⊗ q̄_hat. Conversion to another library must preserve the intended rotation direction and algebra; merely reordering four numbers is insufficient. A known-vector rotation and its inverse provide useful checks.

Trajectory reference frame

A trajectory may track the IMU, camera, or ground-truth marker. Their separated origins follow different position paths during rotation. Apply the known rigid transform so estimates and references describe the same physical frame. Also record whether the output uses a locally continuous odometry frame or a globally corrected map frame.

  • ROS REP 105: odometry and map frame semantics
WORKED EXAMPLE

If T_GI maps IMU coordinates into G and T_IC maps camera coordinates into I, how is a camera point mapped into G?

Explanation

T_GC = T_GI T_IC. Apply T_IC first and T_GI second. If calibration instead supplies T_CI, invert it before composition.
