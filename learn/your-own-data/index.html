Data acquisition and experimental setup

Prepare an inspectable sensor recording, run a known configuration, and keep the evidence needed to diagnose and reproduce the result.

Learning objectives
  • Check sensor content before tuning an estimator
  • Separate raw data, configuration, and generated outputs
  • Distinguish successful execution from demonstrated accuracy

Prerequisites: Camera–IMU calibration and synchronization · VIO initialization · Trajectory error and evaluation coverage

Inspect the sensor recording

Retain the raw recording unchanged. Check image encoding, resolution, camera ordering, shutter/exposure behavior, blur, and feature visibility. Inspect timestamp units, monotonicity, actual sample intervals, overlapping streams, and gaps. Verify gyroscope and accelerometer units and frame conventions; accelerometers report specific force, so a stationary sensor generally does not read zero on every axis.

Choose an initial test with understandable motion and a startup interval suitable for the selected initializer. Calibration data has a separate purpose: collect motion that excites the parameters being estimated while preserving image quality. Moderate motion alone is not a guarantee that every calibration parameter is observable.

  • OpenVINS: camera and IMU calibration
  • Yang, Geneva, Zuo, and Huang, Online Self-Calibration for Visual-Inertial Navigation: Models, Analysis, and Degeneracy (T-RO 2023)

Bind the recording to a supported sensor model

Provide the estimator with its supported camera model, intrinsics, spatial and temporal calibration, and IMU noise parameters. Verify transform direction, quaternion convention, and time-offset sign. Continuous-time noise density and discrete sample variance require different units and sampling-rate treatment. Image resizing or rectification must be reflected in the calibration and selected camera model.

OpenVINS separates estimator options, IMU properties, and camera–IMU calibration in its configuration files. Preserve launch-time overrides and enabled online-calibration states. The Yang et al. analysis explains why some motions leave parameters unobservable; enabling online calibration does not guarantee convergence or replace checking the initial calibration.

  • OpenVINS: camera and IMU calibration
  • OpenVINS: running an example and configuring sensors
  • Yang, Geneva, Zuo, and Huang, Online Self-Calibration for Visual-Inertial Navigation: Models, Analysis, and Degeneracy (T-RO 2023)

Run an upstream baseline and retain the evidence

Reproduce a supported upstream example to check the installation, then change the recording and sensor configuration deliberately. Save the source revision, environment, exact command, resolved settings, input interval, initialization status, logs, and trajectory. Keep incomplete attempts in the run record with their observed outcome.

OpenVINS evaluation uses text beginning with time in seconds, position, and quaternion components qx qy qz qw. Declare position units, pose direction, physical frame, and whether output is online or revised later. Field order alone does not specify a quaternion multiplication convention or perform a sensor-frame conversion.

  • OpenVINS: running an example and configuring sensors
  • OpenVINS: recording and evaluating estimator outputs

State what the recording demonstrates

With suitable reference measurements, evaluate the common-frame trajectories under the declared association, alignment, and error conventions. Document reference quality and availability. A smooth plotted path alone establishes neither absolute accuracy nor a measured drift rate; reference-free checks can still expose gaps, implausible motion, timing problems, and inconsistent repeated behavior.

Save a short experiment record describing the sensor and calibration source, input identity, configuration, output type, attempted runs, evaluation settings, and known gaps. Make each reported number traceable to its input and output. Keep the installation check, successful initialization, completed execution, and demonstrated accuracy as distinct observations.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Your own recording produces a smooth trajectory but has no ground truth. What is a defensible first conclusion?

Explanation

The estimator produced the observed trajectory under the recorded configuration. Confirm initialization and completion from the logs, then report observed continuity and timing. A smooth plot without suitable reference measurements does not establish position accuracy or a measured drift rate.
