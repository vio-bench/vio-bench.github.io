Bring your own camera–IMU recording

Prepare an inspectable sensor recording, run a known configuration, and keep the evidence needed to diagnose and reproduce the result.

Before you begin: Calibrate geometry, noise, and time · Understand what initialization must recover · Read trajectory error together with coverage

Start from an understandable recording

Keep an unchanged copy of the raw recording. Inspect images and IMU samples before connecting them to VIO. Confirm image encoding, dimensions, exposure behavior, and camera ordering. Check timestamp units, monotonicity, stream overlap, missing samples, and actual sample intervals. Plot all gyroscope and accelerometer axes; verify their physical units and frame definitions.

Choose an initial recording whose environment and motion you can explain. Include a usable startup interval for the chosen initializer, textured views, and moderate movement. This makes it easier to distinguish integration problems from genuinely difficult sensing conditions.

Bind the recording to its calibration

Use camera projection and distortion models supported by the selected estimator. Supply intrinsics, camera–IMU transforms, temporal calibration, and IMU noise in that estimator's expected convention. Check any image preprocessing against its calibration. A configuration copied from a public dataset describes that dataset's sensors, not your own hardware.

The OpenVINS tutorial separates estimator settings, IMU properties, and camera–IMU calibration into configuration files. Follow the upstream guide for the exact release and interface you use, and retain the resolved configuration after launch-time overrides.

Run a baseline and preserve outputs

First reproduce a supported upstream example to check the installation. Then change the data and sensor configuration deliberately. Record the command, software revision, environment, input interval, and initialization status. Save logs and the estimated trajectory even when the run is incomplete; a missing output is evidence to diagnose.

For a portable trajectory, evo documents the TUM text layout: timestamp in seconds, position, then quaternion components q_x q_y q_z q_w. Record position units and the pose frame separately. Format conversion does not by itself convert a camera trajectory into an IMU trajectory.

State what the experiment establishes

With a suitable reference, evaluate common-frame trajectories under an explicit association and alignment policy. Without ground truth, a visually plausible path establishes neither absolute accuracy nor a drift rate. You can still inspect continuity, tracking, startup, timing, and whether repeated tests behave consistently.

Package a short README with the sensor model, calibration provenance, input description, commands, outputs, and known gaps. A second person should be able to identify what was measured and reproduce the same experiment without guessing which files belong together.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Your own recording produces a smooth trajectory but has no ground truth. What is a defensible first conclusion?

Reveal the explanation

The estimator initialized and produced the observed output under the recorded configuration. You can report observed continuity and timing, but cannot claim a measured position accuracy or drift rate without a suitable reference.
