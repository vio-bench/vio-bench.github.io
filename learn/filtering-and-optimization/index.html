Prerequisites: VIO initialization

Start with a probabilistic estimation problem

A visual residual compares a measured feature location with its prediction. IMU measurements support propagation in an EKF or motion factors in a batch formulation. Under a conditionally independent Gaussian factor model with fixed covariances, a maximum a posteriori estimate combines a prior with covariance-weighted inertial and visual residuals. A residual covariance is not the posterior state covariance.

The schematic objective below includes the prior in the residual index. Rotations require local perturbations rather than ordinary vector addition. Robust losses, outlier rejection, and which measurements are retained are additional design choices. The estimator family alone does not specify these choices.

An MSCKF retains correlated pose clones

OpenVINS maintains the current IMU orientation, position, velocity, and biases, together with selected past IMU pose clones. These clones retain orientation and position, not a complete historical IMU state at every image. Their cross-covariances let visual constraints on past poses update the correlated current state.

An MSCKF feature is triangulated for the measurement update and eliminated from the linearized constraint; it need not become a persistent landmark state. OpenVINS can additionally retain SLAM landmarks and estimate selected calibration parameters. Report the enabled state variables and clone/landmark limits for the configuration being compared.

Eliminate the feature, retain its motion constraint

For one track, the stacked linearized residual depends on retained-state error δx and feature-position error δp_f. A basis N for the left nullspace of H_f removes the feature term. The projected noise covariance must also be carried into the update; projecting the residual does not make its uncertainty disappear.

For the same whitened linear least-squares system, eliminating an unconstrained feature by an orthonormal nullspace projection or by a Schur complement gives the same reduced quadratic in the retained variables when the whitened feature Jacobian has full column rank. This is a conditional algebraic equivalence. Different priors, measurements, linearization points, or later relinearization produce different estimation problems.

Marginalization fixes part of the linearization history

A sliding-window optimizer can repeatedly linearize active nonlinear factors. Removing old variables usually replaces their factors with a quadratic prior at the marginalization linearization point. That prior preserves the eliminated linearized information; it does not preserve the ability to relinearize the original nonlinear measurements.

For ordinary VIO under sufficiently informative motion and without an external global reference, global translation and rotation about gravity remain unobservable. Incompatible linearizations can falsely add information in these directions. FEJ uses designated first estimates in the relevant Jacobians to preserve the intended nullspace; it does not freeze the estimated trajectory. Chen et al. show that its application in optimization depends on the marginalization structure and connected factors.

Compare the implemented estimator

Compare camera mode, input rate and resolution, active states, track lengths, landmark counts, calibration settings, solver iterations, and linearization policy. Describe local VIO output separately from loop-closed or retrospectively smoothed output. Accuracy and computation must be measured under documented conditions; they do not follow from the labels filtering and optimization alone.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Two systems use the same estimator family but different image resolutions and feature counts. Can a timing difference isolate the effect of the estimator formulation?

Explanation

No. The input and configuration differences also change computational work. First compare a documented common condition, then vary one setting to study its effect.
