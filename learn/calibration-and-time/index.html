Before you begin: Read coordinate frames without guessing

Calibrate distinct quantities

Camera intrinsics define projection and lens distortion for a particular image geometry. Camera–IMU extrinsics define the rigid rotation and translation between sensors. IMU noise parameters describe uncertainty in the inertial readings and bias evolution. Temporal calibration connects the clocks. Each quantity changes a different part of the measurement model.

Use calibration for the images actually supplied to the estimator. Cropping, resizing, rectification, or changing the camera mode can change how pixels relate to rays. Keep the original sensor settings and the processing steps with the calibration files.

A time offset has a direction

Kalibr defines timeshift_cam_imu in seconds through the equation below. Other software may use the opposite sign or shift the other stream. Translate the definition, rather than transferring an unexplained number. Measurement timestamps should represent acquisition timing consistently; message arrival time can include buffering and transport delays.

Inspect timestamp differences for missing samples, duplicated values, jumps, and bursts. A constant offset cannot repair arbitrary jitter or an incorrect time unit.

t_imu = t_cam + timeshift_cam_imu

Collect motion that informs the calibration

For camera–IMU calibration, Kalibr uses a stationary target while the rigid sensor assembly moves. Vary rotation and translation so the measurements constrain multiple axes. Keep the target visible, use even illumination, and reduce motion blur. Purely repetitive motion can leave parameters weakly constrained even if optimization finishes.

Review the calibration report, including image residuals, IMU residuals, timing intervals, and the plausibility of the recovered sensor transform. Repeat with another recording. Large variation between sessions is a reason to inspect the data and model, not to choose the nicer number.

Noise is not a per-sample tuning constant

A continuous-time noise density and a discrete measurement standard deviation have different units. In Kalibr, gyroscope and accelerometer noise densities use (rad/s)/√Hz and (m/s²)/√Hz; the corresponding bias random-walk parameters use (rad/s²)/√Hz and (m/s³)/√Hz. Kalibr separates rapidly varying measurement noise from slowly changing bias. Its conversion to discrete noise assumes an appropriate sampling and filtering model. Copying a per-sample standard deviation into a noise-density field changes how strongly the estimator trusts the IMU.

Retain the noise characterization, calibration recording, target dimensions, software revision, and generated report together. Online calibration can refine supported parameters, but it still depends on an appropriate model and informative data.

CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

Under Kalibr's convention, a camera timestamp is 10.000 s and the offset is +0.004 s. Which IMU-clock time represents that image?

Reveal the explanation

10.004 s. The offset is added to the camera timestamp. Check the destination estimator's definition before reusing that value.
