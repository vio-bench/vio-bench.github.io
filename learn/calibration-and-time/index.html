Camera–IMU calibration and synchronization

Spatial calibration, clock offsets, IMU noise parameters, and the motion conditions needed to estimate them.

Prerequisites: Coordinate frames and pose conventions

Calibration quantities

Camera intrinsics specify projection and distortion. Camera–IMU extrinsics specify the rigid sensor transform. IMU intrinsics model effects such as scale errors and axis misalignment; these are distinct from measurement-noise and bias-evolution parameters. Temporal calibration relates camera and IMU timestamps. The active model determines which quantities are fixed and which are estimated.

Calibration must match the input images. Cropping, resizing, rectification, or a changed camera mode can alter the relation between pixels and rays. Retain the image geometry, sensor settings, and processing steps with the calibration.

  • Yang, Geneva, Zuo, and Huang, Online Self-Calibration for Visual-Inertial Navigation: Models, Analysis, and Degeneracy (T-RO 2023)

Time-offset convention

OpenVINS and Kalibr define the offset in seconds by t_imu = t_cam + t_off. Kalibr names it timeshift_cam_imu. Thus a positive offset maps an image timestamp to a later value on the IMU clock. Software using another definition may require a sign conversion.

Timestamps should consistently describe acquisition time. Message-arrival times can include buffering and transport delays. Inspect missing samples, duplicate timestamps, jumps, and irregular intervals. A constant offset cannot correct arbitrary timing jitter or a wrong time unit.

Motion excitation and calibration evidence

For target-based camera–IMU calibration, keep the target stationary and move the rigid sensor assembly through varied orientations and translations while preserving target visibility and limiting blur. Review image and IMU residuals, timestamp intervals, and the recovered transform’s physical plausibility. Repeatability across recordings provides additional evidence.

Yang, Geneva, Zuo, and Huang’s full self-calibration analysis establishes calibration observability under fully excited six-axis motion for the studied model. Degenerate motions can leave particular parameters unobservable. This condition is model-specific: optimizer convergence, or motion around one axis, does not establish that every calibration parameter was identified. Online refinement also requires informative measurements.

Continuous and discrete noise

In the Kalibr/OpenVINS model, gyroscope and accelerometer white-noise densities have units (rad/s)/√Hz and (m/s²)/√Hz. The corresponding bias-driving random-walk densities have units (rad/s²)/√Hz and (m/s³)/√Hz. These are different quantities from a per-sample measurement standard deviation.

For sample interval Δt in seconds, the idealized model below maps a gyroscope noise density σ_g to a discrete measurement standard deviation σ_g,d. The bias increment standard deviation σ_bg,d scales differently. Analogous expressions hold for the accelerometer. The white-noise conversion assumes appropriate filtering and sampling; simply discarding samples does not establish those assumptions. Preserve the characterization procedure and sensor filtering settings.

WORKED EXAMPLE

With t_cam = 10.000 s and t_off = +0.004 s under the OpenVINS/Kalibr convention, which IMU-clock time corresponds to the image?

Explanation

10.004 s. Add the offset to the camera timestamp. This sign follows the stated equation; it should not be inferred from the parameter name alone.
