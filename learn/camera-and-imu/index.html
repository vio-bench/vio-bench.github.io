Camera and inertial measurements

Visual-inertial odometry (VIO) estimates six-degree-of-freedom (6-DoF) motion: three position and three orientation degrees of freedom. A camera supplies image observations. A gyroscope measures angular velocity, and an accelerometer measures specific force. These measurements constrain a navigation state that also includes velocity and sensor biases.

In a calibrated pinhole model, a point with camera coordinates (x, y, z) projects to normalized coordinates (x/z, y/z). One image therefore provides a bearing, not an independent depth measurement. Observations across images relate scene geometry to camera motion; camera intrinsics then connect normalized coordinates to pixels.

Specific force and the gravity convention

Let G denote the local global frame and I the IMU frame. R_IG maps vectors from G to I. Following OpenVINS, define g_G = [0, 0, g]ᵀ with g approximately 9.81 m/s²; physical gravitational acceleration is −g_G. Let a_G be the IMU origin’s physical acceleration expressed in G. The model below assumes that IMU scale and axis-misalignment effects have already been corrected.

The measured specific force a_m, accelerometer bias b_a, and noise n_a are expressed in I, in m/s². A stationary sensor ideally reads R_IG g_G. Rotating the bias-corrected reading into G and subtracting g_G recovers physical acceleration. A fixed subtraction along one sensor axis is incorrect when the sensor rotates.

Inertial propagation and visual updates

For the calibrated gyroscope model, ω_m = ω_I + b_g + n_g, with angular velocity and bias in rad/s. IMU integration propagates orientation, velocity, and position between visual updates; bias and noise produce accumulating errors. Image measurements constrain this propagation through the camera model.

Monocular visual–inertial estimation can recover metric scale under sufficiently informative motion and appropriate calibration. This is not guaranteed for every trajectory. Weak image geometry, uninformative motion, or incorrect timing can leave the estimate poorly constrained.

Odometry and map correction

Local odometry can accumulate drift. Visual–inertial SLAM may additionally use mapped landmarks, loop closures, or prior-map information to constrain long-term estimates. Retaining some feature states does not by itself establish a global loop-closure capability. For evaluation, identify the output trajectory and whether global corrections were applied.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A stationary, level IMU reports approximately 9.81 m/s² on its upward axis. Does this imply upward motion?

Explanation

No. Ignoring bias and noise, the reading is specific force. Under this lesson’s OpenVINS convention, R_IGᵀ a_m − g_G is zero at rest. The symbol g_G denotes [0, 0, g]ᵀ; the physical gravitational acceleration is −g_G.
