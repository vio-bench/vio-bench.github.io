Camera and IMU measurement models

Understand what each sensor measures and how their information supports six-degree-of-freedom motion estimation.

Learning objectives
  • Distinguish image observations from inertial measurements
  • Explain why an accelerometer does not directly measure position
  • Separate local odometry from globally corrected SLAM

Start with the measurements

Visual-inertial odometry (VIO) estimates six-degree-of-freedom (6-DoF) motion: three components of position and three of orientation. A camera observes the scene; it does not output the camera's motion directly. A gyroscope measures angular velocity, while an accelerometer measures specific force. An estimator must relate these measurements through a motion model and a camera model.

Tracking the same visible point across images constrains how the camera moved relative to the scene. In a pinhole model, projection divides a point's horizontal and vertical coordinates by its depth. This division explains why a single image gives a viewing direction rather than an independent distance measurement.

Gravity is part of the model

Let R_IW rotate world-frame vectors into the IMU frame. The idealized accelerometer equation below uses world acceleration a_W, world gravity g_W, accelerometer bias b_a, and noise n_a. Acceleration and specific force have units of m/s². Angular velocity uses rad/s.

A stationary accelerometer normally has a nonzero reading because its specific force balances gravity. Subtracting a fixed number from one sensor axis is insufficient when the sensor rotates. Gravity must be expressed in the correct frame.

f_m = R_IW (a_W − g_W) + b_a + n_a

Prediction and correction

IMU integration predicts orientation, velocity, and position between image updates. Bias and noise accumulate during integration. Visual observations constrain that prediction when enough informative image structure remains visible. With appropriate calibration and sufficiently informative motion, the combination can recover metric scale even when only one camera is used.

Neither sensor eliminates the other's limitations. Blur or poor texture can weaken image constraints; incorrect biases, timing, or calibration can corrupt the prediction. The estimated trajectory is the result of reconciling imperfect measurements.

VIO and SLAM outputs

VIO produces local motion estimates that can drift. A larger SLAM system may additionally recognize revisited places and correct a global map or pose graph. When comparing outputs, identify whether loop closure or relocalization was enabled and whether the trajectory contains those corrections.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A stationary, level IMU reports approximately 9.81 m/s² on its upward axis. Does this imply upward motion?

Explanation

No. Ignoring bias and noise, a stationary sensor measures the specific force opposing gravity. World acceleration is recovered by rotating specific force into the world and adding the world gravity vector.
