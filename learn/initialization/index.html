VIO initialization

The initial navigation state, stationary and dynamic startup assumptions, and limits imposed by observability.

Initial state and uncertainty

The OpenVINS IMU state contains a global-to-IMU unit quaternion, IMU position and velocity in G, and gyroscope and accelerometer biases. It stores 16 scalar entries, but its local error state has 15 dimensions: three orientation errors and four three-component vector errors. The 6-DoF pose is only part of this state. Cloned poses, features, and estimated calibration parameters enlarge the complete filter state.

Initialization provides estimates and their uncertainty. For visual–inertial estimation without global references, three global translations and rotation about gravity are unobservable under generic, sufficiently informative motion. Degenerate motion may introduce further unobservable directions. Setting an initial position and yaw chooses a coordinate convention; it does not obtain absolute position or heading from the sensors.

Stationary initialization

A stationary interval supports gravity alignment and gyroscope-bias estimation when its assumptions hold. OpenVINS uses an earlier stationary window and can require a subsequent acceleration change before starting. It initializes zero velocity and position, obtains orientation from average acceleration, and assigns biases consistently with that choice.

One static orientation cannot independently identify arbitrary accelerometer bias and tilt. Small measurement variation also does not prove zero motion: constant velocity or constant acceleration with unchanged orientation can produce steady IMU readings. Gravity constrains tilt under the stationary assumptions; it does not determine yaw.

Dynamic initialization

Geneva and Huang’s OpenVINS initialization report combines feature tracks with inertial preintegration over a time window. An initial solve in the first IMU frame estimates velocity, gravity direction, and feature geometry subject to known gravity magnitude. Nonlinear maximum-likelihood refinement then improves the state, with covariance recovery for the estimator that follows.

The initial construction uses supplied bias values and assumes camera calibration and camera–IMU extrinsics are reasonably accurate. Subsequent refinement does not guarantee recovery from arbitrary calibration errors. A short startup interval may not sufficiently constrain all calibration parameters. Weak parallax, interrupted tracks, or poorly excited motion can prevent a well-conditioned solution; faster movement can also increase blur.

Initialization diagnostics

Check delivery of both sensor streams, timestamp units and offset, calibration, and the chosen startup interval. Inspect feature tracks, initialization status, and the first estimated state. Record when initialization succeeds so a failure to start is distinguished from later tracking loss.

Preserve the original recording. When testing a different start time or assumption, record the interval and configuration. A successful return from the initializer is evidence of completing that procedure, not independent verification of calibration or subsequent trajectory accuracy.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Why can a gravity-aligned stationary IMU still have arbitrary initial yaw?

Explanation

A rotation about the gravity direction leaves the stationary gravity observation unchanged. Gravity alignment therefore cannot identify yaw. Without a heading reference, an initial yaw is a local coordinate choice.
