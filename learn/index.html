TUTORIALS / STUDY GUIDE

Studying visual–inertial odometry

A reading sequence through original papers, official derivations, course material, and implementation documentation.

Sources and study sequence

The study guides organize the original OpenVINS tutorials and derivations, together with papers by Guoquan Huang, Patrick Geneva, Chuchu Chen, Yulin Yang, and their coauthors. Each guide identifies specific source sections and a suggested reading order.

For probability, linear algebra, and three-dimensional geometry, begin with the RiSE course, Lectures 1–2. Follow each source’s notation and assumptions; the notation references identify the relevant definitions in OpenVINS.

For implementation, see the implementation guides and system documentation. Dataset downloads, calibration tools, and further reading are collected in Resources.

01

Camera and IMU measurement models

Readings on the visual–inertial state, sensor measurements, and the distinction between odometry and SLAM.

02

Coordinate frames and pose conventions

OpenVINS references for coordinate frames, camera–IMU geometry, quaternion conventions, and orientation errors.

03

Camera–IMU calibration and synchronization

Calibration procedures, parameter definitions, and the motion assumptions examined in the original documentation and calibration analysis.

04

VIO initialization

Primary references on the initialization problem, stationary startup, dynamic initialization, and subsequent state refinement.

05

Filtering and optimization

Read the original treatments of feature elimination, observability, marginalization, and measurement relinearization.

06

Trajectory evaluation

Read the documented trajectory conventions, error definitions, and EPICA evaluation workflow before comparing estimates.

07

Runtime and platform measurements

Read how OpenVINS records timing and process resources, then inspect the measurement scope stated in the original experiments.

08

Working with your own sensor data

Follow the OpenVINS calibration and configuration documentation, then read its trajectory export and file-format conversion guidance.

Further reading by topic

The guides above provide an initial reading sequence. The sources below extend it to mathematical foundations, visual geometry, preintegration, and learning methods. Each entry points to the original material; the current guides are not a complete course.
