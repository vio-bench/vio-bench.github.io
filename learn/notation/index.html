1.1 VIO State and Coordinate Frames

Visual–inertial odometry (VIO) combines camera and inertial measurements to estimate the metric six-degree-of-freedom (6-DoF) motion of a sensing platform: three-dimensional position and orientation. Images constrain motion through observations of the surrounding scene, while IMU measurements constrain motion between image timestamps.

We describe the platform motion using three coordinate frames: the global frame {G}\{G\}{G}, the IMU frame {I}\{I\}{I}, and the camera frame {C}\{C\}{C}. The global frame provides the coordinates in which we express the estimated trajectory; the IMU and camera frames move with their respective sensors.

A representative inertial navigation state is

xI=(GIR, GpI, GvI, bg, ba).\mathbf{x}_I=\left({}^{I}_{G}\mathbf{R},\ {}^{G}\mathbf{p}_{I},\ {}^{G}\mathbf{v}_{I},\ \mathbf{b}_{g},\ \mathbf{b}_{a}\right).xI​=(GI​R, GpI​, GvI​, bg​, ba​).

Here, GpI{}^{G}\mathbf{p}_{I}GpI​ and GvI{}^{G}\mathbf{v}_{I}GvI​ are the IMU position and velocity expressed in the global frame. The rotation matrix GIR{}^{I}_{G}\mathbf{R}GI​R maps vector coordinates from the global frame into the IMU frame. The vectors bg\mathbf{b}_{g}bg​ and ba\mathbf{b}_{a}ba​ are the gyroscope and accelerometer biases. Velocity and biases are estimated alongside pose because they affect the integration of inertial measurements.

The frame labels specify how each quantity should be read. For a position, the superscript identifies the frame in which its coordinates are expressed, and the subscript identifies the object. Thus, GpI{}^{G}\mathbf{p}_{I}GpI​ is the position of the IMU origin expressed in {G}\{G\}{G}. For a rotation, the lower frame label is the source and the upper label is the destination.

For example, an environment point fff is transformed from global coordinates into IMU coordinates as

Ipf=GIR(Gpf−GpI).{}^{I}\mathbf{p}_{f}={}^{I}_{G}\mathbf{R}\left({}^{G}\mathbf{p}_{f}-{}^{G}\mathbf{p}_{I}\right).Ipf​=GI​R(Gpf​−GpI​).

Subtracting the IMU position first expresses the point relative to the IMU origin; the rotation then expresses that displacement along the IMU axes.

1.2 Coordinate Transformations

A rigid-body coordinate transformation combines rotation and translation in one matrix. We write BAT{}^{A}_{B}\mathbf{T}BA​T for the transform that maps point coordinates from frame {B}\{B\}{B} into frame {A}\{A\}{A}. Appending a 1 to a three-dimensional point gives its homogeneous coordinates. These operations change how the same point is expressed in different frames. Modern Robotics, Sec. 3.3.1.
